If you have a pile of Pokémon cards at home and a player who isn't quite ready for the full trading card game, this one is for you.

I first made these rules for my son's birthday party. We wanted every guest to be able to jump in and battle — little siblings, grandparents, friends who had never held a card before. The official game is amazing, but it has a lot to learn. So we stripped it down to the parts that make it fun: picking your favorite Pokémon, powering it up, and cheering when the big number wins.

All Trainers Battle is built for every age (3 and up) and every ability. It works for young kids, for older players with learning or physical disabilities, and for grown-ups who just want an easy way in. Pick from three difficulties — Easy, Medium, or Challenge — and three game lengths, so the same cards grow right along with the player, all the way to the real game.

What you need

Players, cards & a table

  • 2 or more players, ages 3 and up. Little ones can play with a helper.
  • Pokémon cards — any cards with an HP number work.
  • Energy cards — the more, the merrier.
  • Trainer cards (optional) — no reading required, we'll show you how.
  • For Medium and Challenge: a few dice, coins, or a pencil to track damage. The free printables include scorecards for every level. (Dice also come in many Pokémon collection boxes!)
Before you play

Get to know your card

1 HP
The big number at the top right. It shows how strong your Pokémon is.
2 Type symbol
The small symbol by the HP — a flame, a water drop, a leaf, and more. Match it with Energy for a bigger boost!
3 Attacks
Listed in the middle. The symbols on the left are the Energy cost (used in Challenge), and the number on the right is the damage. You'll use attacks in Medium and Challenge.
4 Weakness
Near the bottom. If an attacker's type shows here, its attacks hit twice as hard (Medium and Challenge).

Every version starts the same way

  1. Pokémon stack: each player picks 5, 10, or as many Pokémon as they like.
  2. Energy pile: for Easy, set aside about half as many Energy cards as Pokémon. For Medium and Challenge, use about 1 Energy per Pokémon, since you attach one every turn. More is totally fine!
  3. Trainer cards (optional): add 1 Trainer card for every 5 Pokémon. Each one is a one-time +30 HP boost — no reading needed.
  4. Choose or Surprise? Pick each Pokémon you bring into battle, or shuffle your stack and flip the top card. Works in every version.
Easy ★ · Start here

HP Showdown

Perfect for first-time players, pre-readers, and anyone who loves a big number.

  1. Battle! Everyone puts one Pokémon face up at the same time. Choose your card, or shuffle and flip the top one for a surprise.
  2. Power up (optional): add 1 Energy card from your pile.
  3. Trainer boost (optional): play a Trainer card. Each Trainer card is used once.
  4. Add it up. The highest total HP wins the round! A tie? Everyone high-fives.
  5. Clear the table. Used cards go into your own "done" pile. Everyone keeps their own cards.

Medium ★★ · Ready for more?

Attack Battle

This is how the real game works, made simpler. Pokémon trade attacks until one runs out of HP.

  1. Battle spot: each player puts one Pokémon in front of them.
  2. Take turns. On your turn, attach 1 Energy to your Pokémon. Energy stays attached for the rest of the battle.
  3. Attack! Pick one attack and do that much damage. Does your Pokémon have more than one attack? Choose any one each turn. In Medium, ignore the Energy symbols next to an attack — every attack is ready to use. Skip any extra words on the attack. No number? It does 10 damage.
  4. Track damage. Subtract the damage from the other Pokémon's HP. Dice, coins, or paper all work.
  5. Knocked out! When a Pokémon's HP hits 0, it's knocked out. Its Energy goes back into its Trainer's Energy pile, so you won't run out. Bring out your next Pokémon.
Medium bonuses: Attacks do +10 damage if the Pokémon has matching Energy attached. And if the defending Pokémon shows the attacker's type next to Weakness, the attack does double damage. Trainer card: on your turn, play one to heal your Pokémon 30 HP (up to its starting HP).
Winning: Knock out 3 Pokémon (Quick) or all of the other player's Pokémon (Classic and Marathon).
Challenge ★★★ · Almost the real game

Bench Battle

Plays like Medium, with three real-game skills added: a Bench, Energy costs, and switching. Matching Energy is how you power attacks here, so there's no +10 bonus. Trainer cards still heal 30 HP.

  1. Battle spot & Bench: put one Pokémon in your battle spot and up to 3 more on your Bench, face up beside it.
  2. Energy: on your turn, attach 1 Energy to any of your Pokémon — in battle or on the Bench.
  3. Switch (optional): once per turn, swap your battle Pokémon with one on your Bench. Energy stays with each Pokémon.
  4. Pay the Energy cost: each attack shows symbols on its left. Your Pokémon needs that many Energy attached before it can use that attack, and colored symbols need matching Energy. The plain star symbol can be any Energy. Bigger attacks often need 2 or 3 Energy, so they can take a few turns to power up — that's what the Bench is for! Not enough Energy yet? Use a smaller attack, switch, or end your turn. One attack per turn.
  5. Attack! Subtract damage and use Weakness (double damage). Knocked out? Its Energy goes back into your Energy pile. Move a Bench Pokémon into battle, then refill your Bench from your stack.
Winning: Knock out 3 Pokémon (Quick) or all of the other player's Pokémon (Classic and Marathon).
Want it closer to the real game? When a Pokémon is knocked out, put it and its Energy in a discard pile instead of returning the Energy to your pile — just like the official game.
Mix it up

Ways to play

🤝

Team Up

Everyone works together against one big "Boss" Pokémon picked by a grown-up. Add up the team's damage each turn until the Boss is knocked out. Nobody loses!

🎨

Favorite Color Bonus

If your Pokémon is your favorite color, it gets +30 HP. Tell everyone your favorite color before you start.

👀

No-Reading Mode

Just match symbols and compare numbers. A helper can point to the HP and count along. Energy matching becomes a color-and-shape match game.

✋

Helping Hands

A card stand, a playmat, or sleeved cards make holding and flipping easier. Players can also point to their choice and let a helper place the card.

For grown-ups & helpers

Tips to make it magic

  • Use big battle voices and silly sound effects.
  • Grab stuffed animals or action figures and act out the battles.
  • Ask, "Why do you think your Pokémon is strong?" It builds storytelling and confidence.
  • Let players make up their own attack names.
  • Celebrate great Energy matches and smart choices, not just wins.
  • Keep favorite cards safe in sleeves so nobody worries about bent corners.
